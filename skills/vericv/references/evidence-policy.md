# Evidence and factual-integrity policy

## Source hierarchy

1. CV and portfolio/project material supplied by the candidate.
2. Optional candidate profile explicitly provided for this task.
3. Nothing else.

The job description, common industry practice, inferred timelines, and model knowledge are never candidate evidence.

## Claim rules

Every material tailored-CV claim must point to a source section, role, project, or profile statement. Preserve scope and certainty:

- `Built a Python data-cleaning script` may become `Built data-cleaning scripts in Python`.
- It may not become `Data Engineer`, `expert Python developer`, or `reduced processing time by 40%` without those facts.

Never add or alter employers, locations, job titles, dates, qualifications, certifications, technologies, years, metrics, budgets, team sizes, responsibilities, achievements, contact details, or links. Do not merge separate roles in a way that makes dates or responsibility scope misleading.

## Validation pass

For each material output claim:

1. Locate a source statement.
2. Check entity identity (employer/project), time, technology, role, and metric.
3. Check that the rewrite does not broaden the responsibility or outcome.
4. Mark **Supported**, **Supported with wording adjustment**, or **Remove / ask candidate**.
5. Remove unsupported claims before delivery; do not bury them in a disclaimer.

## Ambiguity handling

Use calibrated wording such as `contributed to`, `supported`, or `worked with` only when the source supports that scope. If the source has no relevant fact, put the requirement under Not Evidenced. A candidate profile may add a fact only when the candidate explicitly states it; label profile-derived evidence in the report.

## Sensitive data

Minimize quoted personal information in reports. Do not expose full addresses, identification numbers, or unnecessary contact details. Keep files local unless the user expressly authorizes another destination.
